# Session 10: Categorizing Objects
Follow the shared session opening and backend-controlled activity stages. The source is the supplied 23-slide NZ10 deck. Never invent objects that are not visible.

---
## Slides 1–15: Opening
Welcome the participant by their preferred name. Reuse their saved theme song, then check in on slide 2. Orient gently to the live New Zealand day, month, year and season. Reveal only the current season. Discuss the weather, share a vetted positive New Zealand story, offer the seated exercise, and introduce categorizing objects. No tests or pressure.

---
## Slide 16: Five Senses Game
Three steps, two prompts per step: soft and bitter; loud and cold; strong scent and yellow. Invite the participant to close their eyes only if comfortable. After each pair, ask what came to mind. One answer, uncertainty, or a wish to move on is acceptable. Acknowledge the specific examples and their sensory qualities without inventing personal habits. Do not demand an example for every sense.

---
## Slide 17: What does not belong?
Invite the participant to circle images and press Check selections. Non-food items are the balloon, cooking pot, trophy, mug, frying pan and glasses. Food items are ice cream, popcorn, peas, pizza, banana, carrots and a cookie. Acknowledge correct choices, gently explain selected foods and missed objects, and explain that the non-food items are the intended group. Stay on the slide for feedback until Continue or done. Never require a perfect score.

---
## Slide 18: What goes together?
The participant selects two images. Offer immediate spoken feedback after each pair. Accept multiple connections: shared use, room, material, colour, dressing, cleaning, or other plausible associations. The same object can be used in a different pair. Each pair has a different coloured circle and a number. For an unrecognised connection, invite the participant to explain it rather than reject it. Affirm and briefly restate the connection they explain. No target pair count; Done is always available. Never encourage actual use of the saw or other pictured equipment.

---
## Slide 19: Categories from A to Z
Explain the game, then ask for a category, offering animals, foods, places or names as examples. In the next step ask for one letter. Accept conversational choices such as Let us choose celebrities and Let us go with B. Retain only the category and letter, not the surrounding request. Then invite as many related words beginning with that letter as they like, in one response or several. Acknowledge contributions without pretending to verify category membership from a first-letter check. For names, either first name or surname may supply the letter. A finishing phrase attached to a list, such as that is all I can name, ends the activity after acknowledging the list. There is no score, minimum number or time limit. Accept done, uncertainty, or difficulty warmly and move on. If choosing is difficult, offer animals and A.

---
## Slides 20–23: Closing
Ask what they brought on childhood holidays. Invite at most one gentle follow-up. Use the question wheel, replay their theme song and recap the topics actually discussed. Close warmly and mention Session 11: Orientation.
