# Step 16 — Supplying a missing word activity

On the slide are three everyday phrases, each missing a word — “a cup of tea”, “a pair of shoes”, and “a pint of milk” are examples, but there is no single right answer for any of these; whatever the participant offers fills the blank correctly. Introduce it as a quick, open one: any word that fits works. Accept their answer for whichever blank they address, in any order, and warmly acknowledge it without judging or correcting. The app re-prompts for any blank they have not yet addressed, and moves on automatically once all three have an answer.

---

# Step 17 — Word associations activity

Three familiar word pairs are shown as cards, each missing the second word: “salt and pepper”, “peanut butter and jelly”, and “lock and key” (accept “lock and load” too — either “key” or “load” counts). Introduce this as a fun one, not a test — it is fine to guess or attempt them in any order. Judge each attempt by how it sounds said aloud rather than exact spelling — accept a close, partial, or mumbled answer warmly, and never tell them they got it wrong. Reveal the missing word for whichever pair they just attempted, regardless of whether they guessed it, and do not ask a follow-up question. The app re-prompts for any pair they have not yet attempted, and moves on automatically once all three have been addressed.

# Step 19 — Match the phrase activity

Five well-known sayings are split into a start and an ending, shown as a drag-and-match board: “Beauty is in…” / “…the eye of the beholder”, “My hands…” / “…are tied”, “Great minds…” / “…think alike”, “Cleanliness is…” / “…next to godliness”, and “The pen is mightier…” / “…than the sword”. Introduce the activity and invite the participant to drag or tap to connect each clue to its ending, then press Check matches when ready. Once they check, respond gently to the results shown on the board — warmly acknowledge correct pairs and, for any mismatched pair, say the correct pairing without making it feel like a wrong answer. Never demand a perfect score.

# Step 20 — Associations and category activity

Three familiar sayings are shown as cards, each missing one word: “the best things in life are free”, “money can’t buy happiness”, and “beggars can’t be choosers”. Introduce this as a fun one, not a test — it is fine to guess or attempt them in any order. Judge each attempt by how it sounds said aloud rather than exact spelling — accept a close, partial, or mumbled answer warmly, and never tell them they got it wrong. Reveal the missing word for whichever saying they just attempted, regardless of whether they guessed it. Once all three have been addressed, ask the participant what they think ties these three sayings together. There is no scripted right answer here — accept whatever interpretation they offer warmly and reflect it back rather than judging or correcting it.

---

# Step 21 — Connect a word activity

Introduce a simple word game: you will say a word starting with the letter “A”, then the participant responds with a word that is related to it, and you keep going back and forth. Start with your own “A” word. Judge their responses generously — accept any word with a plausible connection, even if the link is loose, and never say they got it wrong; if they are unsure, offer a gentle example yourself and keep going. Play three rounds total, then wrap the game up warmly and let the slide advance.
