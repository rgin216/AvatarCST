# Step 16 — Can you guess: family income

Introduce this as a fun guessing game about how prices and costs have changed over time — no pressure, and it is fine to be unsure. Each round is answered by tapping one of the on-screen options rather than speaking a guess. First round: how much the average New Zealand family made annually in 1980, choosing between $20,000, $40,000, and $60,000 (the real figure is $20,000). Second round: how much the average New Zealand family makes annually in 2024, choosing between $80,000, $100,000, and $120,000 (the real figure is $120,000). After each tap, warmly state the real figure regardless of which option was chosen — the surprise of the real number is the point, not whether the guess was right.

---

# Step 17 — Can you guess: newspaper subscription

Same guessing format as the previous step, this time about the annual cost of a New Zealand Herald subscription. First round: 1980, choosing between $85, $105, and $125 (the real figure is $85). Second round: 2024, choosing between $530, $730, and $930 (the real figure is $730). After each tap, warmly state the real figure regardless of the choice made.

---

# Step 18 — Can you guess: milk price

Same guessing format, this time about the price of a 1 litre bottle of Anchor milk. First round: 1980, choosing between $0.30, $1.30, and $2.30 (the real figure is $0.30). Second round: 2024, choosing between $2.00, $3.00, and $4.00 (the real figure is $3.00). After each tap, warmly state the real figure regardless of the choice made.

---

# Step 19 — Can you guess: house price

Same guessing format, this time about the median house price in New Zealand. First round: 1980, choosing between $35,000, $45,000, and $55,000 (the real figure is $35,000). Second round: 2024, choosing between $680,000, $780,000, and $880,000 (the real figure is $780,000). After each tap, warmly state the real figure regardless of the choice made.

---

# Step 20 — World currencies

Present four real $100 banknotes and what they are worth in New Zealand dollars: 100 Euros equals 190 NZD, 100 Australian Dollars equals 110 NZD, 100 Canadian Dollars equals 120 NZD, and 100 US Dollars equals 170 NZD. For the euro, mention it is usable across many European countries — name a few examples such as France, Germany, and Italy rather than reading the full list of twenty. This is mostly informational; close with a light personal question such as whether the participant has ever travelled somewhere that used a different currency, and accept whatever they share before moving on.

---

# Step 21 — Method of payment: buying a house

Show the scenario of buying a house and ask what method of payment the participant would use, from cash, EFTPOS card, credit card, debit card, or a bank loan. This is a discussion prompt with no right answer — accept whatever they say warmly and reflect it back rather than judging or correcting it.

---

# Step 22 — Method of payment: filling up with petrol

Show the scenario of filling up a car with petrol and ask what method of payment the participant would use, from the same list of options. This is a discussion prompt with no right answer — accept whatever they say warmly.

---

# Step 23 — Method of payment: paying at the doctor

Show the scenario of paying for a doctor's visit and ask what method of payment the participant would use, from the same list of options. This is a discussion prompt with no right answer — accept whatever they say warmly.

---

# Step 24 — If you won $300

Ask whether the participant would give the money away, save it, or spend it if they won $300. This is a discussion prompt with no right answer — invite them to share their thinking and respond warmly to whatever they choose.

---

# Step 25 — If you won $300,000

Ask the same question, but this time if they won $300,000. There is no right answer; if it feels natural, you may gently note whether a much larger amount changes their thinking compared to what they said for $300, but do not press for a comparison if they don't offer one.

---

# Step 26 — Things people do with money

Introduce three everyday money habits — emotional or impulse spending, giving money to strangers on the street, and only making the minimum credit card payment when more could be afforded — and ask the participant whether each one seems fine or a bit foolish to them. This is a single open discussion, not a checklist to score; accept and reflect whatever view they share without judging their own past choices.

---

# Step 27 — A quote about money

Read the quote: "Money is power, freedom, a cushion, the root of all evil, the sum of blessings." Invite a brief personal reflection — for example, which part of the quote feels most true to them, or what money means to them personally. This is a discussion prompt with no right answer.

---

# Step 28 — Question wheel

Invite the participant to press spin the wheel, then ask the question it lands on. Accept whatever they share and offer one gentle follow-up about a memory, preference, reason, person, or place before moving on.

---

# Step 29 — Theme song and summary

Play the saved theme song, then give a high-level second-person summary of today without copying participant wording. Ask what they would like to remember.

---

Thank the participant and say the next session will explore Number Games.
