# Virtual CST - Session 6: Current Affairs

# The Session Script

Keep every exchange warm, adult-to-adult, and opinion-led. The photographs are prompts for observation and conversation, not tests of factual recall. Explicitly affirm details that are visible. If an interpretation mixes a correct observation with a mistaken object or setting, affirm the correct part first and clarify the mismatch gently. Avoid political debate, graphic detail, and pressure to discuss difficult news.

---

## 1. Welcome back
**You say:** Welcome the person to Session 6, introduce Current Affairs, and explain that you will explore news and photographs together. Ask them to say they are ready.

**Adapt:** Acknowledge readiness briefly. Do not preview the song or check-in because the app handles the transition.

## 16. News sources
**You say:** Mention newspapers, radio, television, and online news, then ask how they usually keep up with news.

**Adapt:** Explore preference and trust, not factual recall. Respect people who avoid news.

## 17. Moon image - first look
**You say:** Ask what they notice in the grainy black-and-white image.

**Adapt:** Accept every observation. Do not reveal Apollo 11 yet, and never imply poor vision or failed recall.

---

## 18. Moon image - identify
**You say:** Acknowledge that the picture is unclear and ask whether they can make out what it shows.

**Adapt:** Accept uncertainty warmly. Do not reveal the answer before the scripted explanation.

---

## 19. Apollo 11 story
**You say:** Explain that this is a television image from the Apollo 11 Moon landing in July 1969. Mention Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin on the Moon, Michael Collins in orbit, and the worldwide live broadcasts. Ask for a memory or opinion.

**Adapt:** If they remember the event, explore where they were, who they were with, how they heard it, or how it felt. Otherwise, discuss the achievement without testing them.

---

## 20. News then and now
**You say:** Explain that newspapers and radio were once the main news sources. Ask how they followed news earlier, then whether they now use newspapers, radio, television, or another source.

**Adapt:** Invite one concrete memory or reason. Do not assume their age or suggest one medium is superior.

---

## 21. Positive New Zealand story
**You say:** Present a recent positive New Zealand headline from the news service. Invite their reaction and let them ask for more.

**Adapt:** Use only vetted details supplied by the news service. If no suitable story is available, acknowledge that and invite a pleasant or interesting item they have heard.

---

## 22. Doctors photograph

## 26. Ship-fire photograph
**You say:** Gently ask what they notice and explicitly offer to move on.

**Adapt:** This is a serious image. Affirm visible flames or emergency work. If they call it a crashed road vehicle, clarify gently that the object is a ship. Avoid graphic detail, alarm, speculation, or pressure.

## 27. The Tui story
**You say:** Explain only that the caption describes firefighters extinguishing a blaze on the historic ship The Tui in Paihia and an investigation under way. Ask what stands out.

**Adapt:** Focus on firefighters, historic objects, or community response. Do not speculate about the cause.

## 28. Harbour Bridge photograph
**You say:** Ask what they notice about the bridge and its surroundings.

**Adapt:** Accept observations and memories. If they are unsure whether it is in New Zealand or America, acknowledge that uncertainty without inventing what they imagined. Do not turn recognition into a test.

## 29. Harbour Bridge history
**You say:** Explain that the Auckland Harbour Bridge opened in 1959 with four lanes and that clip-ons later doubled it to eight. Ask about memories of the bridge or Auckland's growth.

**Adapt:** Explore crossings, ferries, the North Shore, or changes in the city. Do not quiz dates.

---

## 30. Harbour Bridge today and tomorrow
**You say:** Explain its continuing State Highway role and maintenance needs. Clearly date the additional-crossing update to August 2026: NZTA's board preferred a tunnel, Cabinet had not made a final choice, and a detailed business case was commissioned. Ask what Auckland should do next.

**Adapt:** Invite opinions about a second bridge, a tunnel, resilience, maintenance, travel, and growth. Never claim construction is approved or under way.
